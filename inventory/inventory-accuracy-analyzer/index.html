    How the risk score works

    The 0–100 score combines variance exposure (30), annual usage/velocity (20), item value (20), time since last count (15), previous variances (10) and operational criticality (5). Dataset percentiles keep the ranking useful across businesses of different sizes. The score supports professional judgment; it does not replace it.

    ABC method: annual usage × unit cost, ranked by cumulative value. Defaults: A through 80%, B through 95%, C thereafter.

    From discrepancy data to a focused action plan

    What this tool answers

    Which mismatches create the greatest financial or operational risk? Which items should be recounted first? Are a few items driving most of the exposure? The analyzer makes those decisions visible without requiring a WMS or spreadsheet formulas.

    Recommended workflow

    Import a completed count, analyze the priorities, recount critical items independently, review receiving/issues/transfers, document the root cause, then post approved adjustments. Export the result as your review evidence.

    Interpret accuracy carefully

    Exact-match accuracy is intentionally strict. A business may also use quantity or value tolerances, but these should be approved internally and applied consistently. High-value A items normally warrant tighter control.

    Inventory Accuracy Analyzer — FAQs

    Is inventory data uploaded to DIXANI?

    No. CSV parsing, analysis and exports happen in your browser. Refreshing the page removes the working data unless your browser restores the tab.

    Why can an item with zero variance still receive a risk score?

    High-value, fast-moving, overdue or historically inaccurate items may deserve a preventive count even when the current record matches.

    Is the ranking a replacement for internal controls?

    No. It is a decision-support model. Review its reasons against your materiality thresholds, product criticality and operating procedures.

    What does ABC mean?

    ABC separates items by cumulative annual usage value. A items contribute the first 80%, B the next 15%, and C the remaining 5% under the tool’s default method.
